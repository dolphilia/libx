# Awesome Social Enterprise

Social enterprises primarily pursue social impact and fund at least part of their activities themselves. Explore their organizational structures through reading, courses, podcasts and films, or find fellowships, institutions and communities that support them.

Program lengths, funding amounts and community sizes reflect the source snapshot, rather than current availability.

### Papers
* (1998) _The Meaning of "Social Entrepreneurship"_ by J. Gregory Dees
  * A short history of social entrepreneurship (and why the definition isn't agreed upon).
* (2004) _The Legitimacy of Social Enterprise_ by Raymond Dart
  * A pragmatic discussion on why social enterprises, in relation to nonprofits, exist.
* (2008) [_Evolution of the social enterprise industry_](https://socialent.org/documents/EVOLUTIONOFTHESOCIALENTERPRISEINDUSTRY--ACHRONOLOGYOFKEYEVENTS.pdf) by the Institute for Social Entrepreneurs
  * A less academic but detailed chronology of events related to social enterprises.
* (2010) _Conceptions of Social Enterprise and Social Entrepreneurship_ by Jacques Defourny
  * Parallels between social enterprises in the United States and Europe.
* (2014) _Social Enterprise – A New Phenomenon in the Field of Economic and Social Welfare?_ by Leandro Sepulveda
  * A history of social enterprises from a UK perspective.

## FAQs

Q: What can social enterprises offer?\
A: They can combine nonprofits' social purpose with business efficiency to maximize social impact. Not all nonprofits are inefficient, and not all businesses are bad.

Q: Why focus on social enterprise rather than social entrepreneurship?\
A: This collection focuses on organizational structures rather than individuals within an organization. Social entrepreneurship ranges from large organizations championing social causes to individuals doing community development. Both are important and valuable; the focus here is the organizational side.

Q: How is a social enterprise different from a nonprofit?\
A: A social enterprise may be a nonprofit. Nonprofits usually focus on social impact, but they may not generate revenue to fund themselves.
