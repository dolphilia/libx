# Awesome Engineering Team Management

Resources for software developers considering management, first-time team leads, managers navigating daily operations, and those preparing for the next management level. The collection progresses from roles and team building to organizational and behavioral practices, then examines politics, burnout, failure, and departures.

The list draws on its author’s [transition from software engineer to engineering manager](https://devtomanager.com/interviews/kevin-deldycke/) and later to managing other managers. It combines practical advice with anecdotes, discussions, industry commentary, and critical perspectives on the role.

> The manager's function is not to make people work, but to make it possible for people to work.
>
> — Tom DeMarco, [source note](#intro-quote-def)

## Engineering to Management Transition

Moving from an individual contributor (IC) role to front-line management is the first, often hardest transition. These resources discuss how to learn the new role.

- For a developer, [a management position is not a promotion. It is a change in career](https://fractio.nl/2014/09/19/not-a-promotion-a-career-change/).

- [17 Reasons not to be a Manager](https://charity.wtf/2019/09/08/reasons-not-to-be-a-manager/) - A warning about the difficulties of management, intended to [discourage the faint-hearted recruits](https://youtu.be/b07887ZzKiw?t=40).

- [Advice to New Managers: Don't Joke About Firing People](https://staysaasy.com/engineering/2020/06/09/Don%27t-Joke.html) - Warns that becoming someone’s manager means giving up jokes about their employment at the company.

- [Advice to new managers](https://x.com/ValaAfshar/status/966125964861280256) - 9 fundamental principles of the behavior required to be a great manager.

- [Mistakes I've Made as an Engineering Manager](https://css-tricks.com/mistakes-ive-made-as-an-engineering-manager/) - The author identifies four mistakes: assuming people give feedback in the way they want to receive it; trying to do everything alone; assuming one communication is enough; and believing a manager must have everything together all the time.

- [Why It's Easier to Manage 4 People Than It Is to Manage 1 Person](https://staysaasy.com/management/2020/07/24/Managing-One-Person.html) - The author warns against combining four conditions: a new manager, only one direct report, a report who is new to the industry, and a manager who is not a subject-matter expert.

- [Going from Developer to Manager. What should I know or learn?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18823616)

- [How to be a Manager – A step-by-step guide to leading a team](https://getweeklyupdate.com/manager-guide) - A detailed guide to modern management practices.

- [On being an Engineering Manager](https://ruiper.es/posts/engineer-manager-2017/) - Some points need qualification, while others offer first-time managers a preview of the role.

- [Responsibility vs. accountability](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21892816) - A discussion distinguishing a manager’s accountability for outcomes from engineers’ responsibility for doing the work. It argues that the accountable person faces the consequences when things go wrong, while the responsible person can move on to another project.

- “A computer can never be held accountable. Therefore a computer must never make a management decision.” - An [IBM slide from 1979](https://x.com/mit_csail/status/1604884273789603842).

- A [source discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18222488) characterizes management as trying to disappoint people as slowly as possible.

- A [source discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23973859), drawing on [7 habits of highly effective people](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/1982137274), proposes putting direct reports in charge while the manager supports them and responds to their requests. It stresses that the goal must be clear.

- [The One Minute Manager Meets the Monkey](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0688103804) - Uses problems as a metaphorical “monkey”: inexperienced managers accept problems from others until the burden accumulates. The book explains how to delegate responsibility instead of becoming a bottleneck.

## Building Teams

A management title and pay grade do not by themselves make an effective manager. Whether inheriting a team or starting one, managers need to learn how to build and strengthen it.

- [Building and Motivating Engineering Teams](http://www.elidedbranches.com/2016/11/building-and-motivating-engineering.html) - Discusses what engineers want from their work: money, purpose, and respect.

- [What Google Learned From Its Quest to Build the Perfect Team](https://web.archive.org/web/20250601205421/https://www.nytimes.com/2016/02/28/magazine/what-google-learned-from-its-quest-to-build-the-perfect-team.html) - Reports Google’s finding that psychological safety, more than any other factor, was critical to effective teams. Conversational turn-taking and empathy are presented as behaviors that help create that safety and interpersonal bonds.

- [Paper we love: Software Engineering Organizations](https://github.com/papers-we-love/papers-we-love/tree/master/software_engineering_orgs) - “The practice of software engineering, and its history is, itself, a complex study in humanity, coordination, and communication.”

- [Developer Tropes: "Google does it"](https://tomaytotomato.com/developer-tropes-2/) - Warns against [cargo-cultish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_cult) imitation of large technology companies. It suggests that managers act like ecologists: measure, observe, and nurture their own ecosystem to build a workplace suited to it.

## Roles

These resources describe the profiles, attitudes, behaviors, and expectations of developers, managers, and executives, and how their roles relate to one another.

### Executives

Executives occupy the senior management layers of a company. They report to a board of directors in larger companies or directly to shareholders in smaller ones, and are expected to provide leadership. They are also the people to whom managers report.

- [What do executives do, anyway?](https://apenwarr.ca/log/20190926) - Drawing on [Andy Grove’s book, High Output Management](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0394532341), the article describes executives as defining and enforcing organizational culture and values, and ratifying good decisions. It also examines CEO failure modes: imposing decisions downstream and failing to resolve conflicts.

- [Executives ratify decisions made on the spot](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18089716) - Applies Tolstoy’s account of how decisions emerge to business leadership.

- [Army Leadership and the Profession](https://fas.org/irp/doddir/army/adp6_22.pdf) - Establishes and describes what leaders should be and do.

- [US Air Force's Strategic Leadership Studies](https://web.archive.org/web/20190308062113/http://leadership.au.af.mil/sls-skil.htm) - A reference of leadership's competencies and skills.

- [What Only the CEO Can Do](https://archive.ph/CcScN) - Identifies four CEO responsibilities: defining and interpreting the company’s meaningful external context; deciding what business it is and is not in; balancing present returns with future investment; and shaping organizational values and standards.

- [How CEOs Manage Time](https://archive.ph/S32wu) - A study of how CEOs of large companies spend their time, illustrating the components and dimensions of leadership.

- [Operations and Internal Communication Strategies For Effective CEOs](https://archive.ph/9DkfA) - Emphasizes context and narratives, then provides a template for recurring internal communication rituals.

- [Regis McKenna's talk at Silicon Valley Leaders Symposium](https://youtu.be/5Z13NI0SuyA?t=2026) - Regis McKenna argues that marketing activities once carried out by people have become automated, making the CIO the new marketing chief.

- [Narcissistic CEOs Weaken Collaboration and Integrity](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/narcissistic-ceos-weaken-collaboration-integrity) - Warns that the profile of a visionary leader can resemble narcissism, so boards that are not careful may select narcissistic CEOs.

- “Hiring isn't the challenge. The challenge is finding people who can be effective while working for executives whose only qualifications and training are narcissistic levels of self confidence.” ([source](https://x.com/kellan/status/1205113384632500224)).

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24519247) describes how a controlling, micromanaging CEO can intercept financial information before it reaches people who could detect fraud. The list presents this as a reason for board oversight.

### CTO & VP of Engineering

CTOs and VPs of Engineering are critical roles in technology companies, but their responsibilities often overlap and the boundary between them can be unclear.

- [CTO vs VP Engineering: What's the Difference?](https://www.ivyexec.com/career-advice/2015/cto-versus-vp-engineering-whats-the-difference/) - Describes a CTO as managing a small group of technically focused developers, and a VP of Engineering as leading an engineering organization.

- [Want to Know the Difference Between a CTO and a VP Engineering?](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/want-to-know-the-difference-between-a-cto-and-a-vp-engineering-4fc3750c596b) - Compares the roles using a two-axis framework of process orientation and technical capability.

- [The different skills needed to be a successful CTO](https://madewithlove.be/one-job-many-roles-the-different-skills-needed-to-be-a-successful-cto/) - Describes a technical startup founder’s evolving responsibilities as the company grows. The original list questions its framing, arguing that the eventual role described is closer to VP of Engineering than CTO.

- [Hiring a VP of Engineering? Use This Framework](https://review.firstround.com/hiring-a-vp-of-engineering-use-this-framework-from-shopifys-vpe-to-get-it-right/) - “*How do I hire a VP of Engineering?* After more than 20 years, eight companies, and thousands of hires, I'm starting to suspect this may be the wrong question. A better one is, *What is a VP of Engineering?*”

- [“That's usually about the time I nope right out of the interview”](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19188246) - A discussion of warning signs when a CTO recruits an engineering manager, including the risks of refusing to acknowledge management hierarchies.

### Engineering Managers

Management titles and daily responsibilities vary widely between companies. When developers report directly to a manager, that manager occupies the first, front-line level of engineering management.

- [What are the signs that you have a great manager?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20230133) - “The irony is that you don't really notice a great manager.”

- [Identify what makes a great manager](https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/managers-identify-what-makes-a-great-manager#learn-about-googles-manager-research) - Google’s research began as an attempt to show that managers did not matter; it instead identified [10 Traits of the Very Best Ones](https://archive.ph/1USa4).

- [As a product manager, how do you earn the respect and trust of your team?](https://web.archive.org/web/20220115172555/https://twitter.com/johncutlefish/status/1124938723093766144)

- [Good Boss, Bad Boss: A Peek Inside the Minds of the Best (and Worst)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lmBSh1FGQyY) - Describes a good boss as removing destructive people from the team—the “no asshole rule”—and protecting people from poor decisions higher in the organization.

- A [source discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19187593) describes managers as filtering information in both directions and offers advice on deciding what to share or withhold.

- [Great PMs don't spend their time on solutions](https://web.archive.org/web/20250511211605/https://www.intercom.com/blog/great-product-managers-dont-spend-time-on-solutions/) - Argues that product managers should focus on customers’ problems rather than prematurely choosing solutions.

- [Things I have learnt as the software engineering lead of a multinational](https://minnenratta.wordpress.com/2017/01/25/things-i-have-learnt-as-the-software-engineering-lead-of-a-multinational/) - Offers engineering-lead lessons; the original list finds some useful and others worth challenging.

- [Surprising Things About Working at Well-Known Tech Unicorns](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/surprising-things-about-working-at-tech-unicorns/) - Explores the gap between expectations and reality in prominent, rapidly growing companies, echoing the list author’s engineering-management experience.

- [100+ Lessons Learned for Project Managers](https://llis.nasa.gov/lesson/1956) - 122 lessons about NASA project management, covering design, decisions, staff management, and work with superiors and contractors.

- [Engineering Manager Resources](https://github.com/ryanburgess/engineer-manager) - A large collection of engineering-manager resources that benefits from further selection.

- [A vitally important part of the job: being a crap shield](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24802483) - A manager’s account of acting as a “crap shield”: taking on difficult organizational work so the team can concentrate on its job.

### Engineers

- [Programmer Moneyball: Challenging the Myth of Individual Programmer Productivity](https://insights.sei.cmu.edu/sei_blog/2020/01/programmer-moneyball-challenging-the-myth-of-individual-programmer-productivity.html) - Argues that limited ability to evaluate individual developers makes a productive environment and talent development especially important to software project managers.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5496914) argues that “10x” developers can rapidly become “1x” developers or worse if they cannot make their own architectural choices.

- [7 absolute truths I unlearned as junior developer](https://monicalent.com/blog/2019/06/03/absolute-truths-unlearned-as-junior-developer/) - Seven beliefs the author abandoned: being senior already; everyone writing tests; being far behind other teams (technical FOMO); code quality mattering most; documenting everything; technical debt always being bad; and seniority meaning being the best programmer.

- [On Being A Senior Engineer](https://www.kitchensoap.com/2012/10/25/on-being-a-senior-engineer/) - “I expect a 'senior' engineer to be a *mature* engineer.”

- [Things I Learnt from a Senior Software Engineer](https://neilkakkar.com/things-I-learnt-from-a-senior-dev.html) - “I sat next to a senior software engineer for a year. Here's what I learnt.”

- [5 Things I've Learned in 20 Years of Programming](https://daedtech.com/5-things-ive-learned-in-20-years-of-programming/) - The author argues that a programmer with five years of experience has more industry tenure than over half the industry. A follow-up comment offers [10 things I’ve learned after 35 years](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21612990). These are the sources’ perspectives on experience.

- [Devs I really enjoy pairing with](https://x.com/ScribblingOn/status/1002598672444448768) - Describes enjoyable pairing partners as people who avoid acting like know-it-alls, admit what they do not know, and work through problems together.

- [All the best engineering advice I stole from non-technical people](https://medium.com/@bellmar/all-the-best-engineering-advice-i-stole-from-non-technical-people-eb7f90ca2f5f) - “It's intriguing that the stuff that really seems to make a difference in the quality of software never seems to be about software.”

- [What Makes A Great Software Engineer?](https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Li2015GreatEngineers.pdf) - Models software-engineering ability using 53 attributes without claiming a definitive answer to what makes a great engineer. It also references other research on the subject.

- [What makes a Senior Dev](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11341567) - A discussion emphasizing that seniority requires time and experience.

- [The different engineering levels at Google](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24627229) - Brief descriptions of Google engineering levels L3 through L8.

- [How I operated as a Staff engineer at Heroku](https://amyunger.com/blog/2020/09/10/staff-engineer-at-heroku.html) - An account of the sometimes unclear Staff Engineer role, also called Principal Engineer or Software Architect in some organizations. It combines technical expertise with solving engineering problems rooted in social, communication, and organizational complexity.

- [StaffEng](https://staffeng.com) - Guides to the Staff Engineer path: after the Senior Software Engineer level, a developer can pursue engineering management or further technical depth.

- [10 Admirable Attributes of a Great Technical Lead](https://betterprogramming.pub/10-admirable-attributes-of-a-great-technical-lead-251d13a8843b) - “They are smart yet kind. Knowledgeable, yet humble. Busy, yet approachable.”

### Consultants

- “A consultant is someone 4 pages ahead in the manual” ([source](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20786286)).

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21714791) argues that consultants often provide political cover for changes an organization already knows it needs but lacks the social capital or focus to make. The list sees this as a reason consultants find work in bureaucratic, highly political organizations.

- [The Prosperous Software Consultant](https://dabit3.medium.com/the-prosperous-software-consultant-5dc8d705c5dd) - An account of how an independent software consultant works.

## Recruitment

Recruitment can take substantial management time in a competitive sector: managers hire to expand a team or fill vacancies, while also participating in hiring as candidates for their own next roles.

### Job Boards

Choosing where to advertise a role can help reach the candidates it is intended for.

- [Awesome Job Boards](https://github.com/tramcar/awesome-job-boards) - Specialized job boards organized by domain, technology, role, and location.

- [Hiring Without Whiteboards](https://github.com/poteto/hiring-without-whiteboards) - Companies whose interviews avoid the computer-science trivia associated with poor interview practices.

### Hiring Process

High-growth companies eventually need to make hiring a repeatable process.

- [Why I Never Hire Brilliant Men](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Why_I_Never_Hire_Brilliant_Men) - A 1924 essay presenting five hiring rules under its historical title. The original list sees continuity with later hiring practices; its gendered framing belongs to the original period.

- [A Good Tech Resume](https://thetechresume.com/A_Good_Tech_Resume.pdf) - Resume advice and examples, including an explanation of a typical hiring pipeline.

- [Job Interviewing Guide](https://www.homerun.co/artofwork/guides/job-interviewing) - A detailed hiring-process guide that can inform a company beginning to formalize recruitment as it grows.

- [Open Sourced Interview Process](https://github.com/cockroachlabs/open-sourced-interview-process) - Cockroach Labs shares a process designed to make candidates familiar with interviews and account for bias, with the aim of improving both candidate experience and hiring decisions.

- [Rethinking the Hiring Process](https://www.karllhughes.com/posts/rethinking-hiring) - Argues that testing programmers on skills they will not need at work can misrepresent their ability, flatter the hiring team, and favor candidates with traditional computer-science backgrounds.

### Interview

These questions and topics can help assess candidates and start interview conversations.

- [The Technical Interview is an Ego Trip](https://web.archive.org/web/20221101193146/https://kowsheek.com/the-technical-interview-is-an-ego-trip/) - Begins with an anecdote about developers using interviews to display superiority, then describes a practical process intended to respect everyone’s time.

- [The Intangible Skills You Can't Interview For](https://staysaasy.com/leadership/2021/04/12/the-intangible-skills-you-cant-interview-for.html) - Discusses five hard-to-assess skills: cutting through unpleasant tasks, knowing how to finish, knowing how to start, giving and receiving feedback across reporting lines, and harnessing the value of intangibles.

- [Back-End Developer Interview Questions](https://github.com/arialdomartini/Back-End-Developer-Interview-Questions) - Back-end interview questions to use as inspiration.

- [Reverse interview](https://github.com/viraptor/reverse-interview) - Questions candidates can ask a company; managers can also use them to prepare for their own answers.

- [Culture Queries](https://www.keyvalues.com/culture-queries) - Example interview questions for understanding a company’s values.

- [Book Summary of "Who: The A Method for Hiring"](https://medium.com/mbreads/book-summary-who-c4a437d8ae3a) - Summarizes executive-recruitment lessons from [*Who*, a popular book](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0345504194).

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13209317) disputes treating lower hiring standards as the explanation for negative contributions, giving examples of developers who pass difficult interviews but later reduce a team’s value.

### Coding Challenge

The original list frames coding assessment as a trade-off: omitting it can admit candidates who misrepresent their skills, while overly selective challenges can reject capable developers. Managers need to balance these risks and decide how candidates can demonstrate their skills.

- [How to Freaking Find Great Developers By Having Them Read Code](https://web.archive.org/web/20230416055512/https://freakingrectangle.com/2022/04/15/how-to-freaking-hire-great-developers/) - Proposes assessing candidates by reading and discussing existing code. The author says reading accounts for 95% of developers’ work, that the first five minutes can reveal programming skills, and that stress raises adrenaline and lowers IQ by several points, potentially obscuring good candidates. These claims explain the author’s rationale, rather than establishing universal measures.

- [Organizational Skills Beat Algorithmic Wizardry](https://prog21.dadgum.com/177.html) - Argues that organizing interdependent features so they do not collapse under their own complexity matters more than algorithmic prowess.

- [The Horrifically Dystopian World of Software Engineering Interviews](https://web.archive.org/web/20210911031845/https://www.jarednelsen.dev/posts/The-horrifically-dystopian-world-of-software-engineering-interviews) - Examines the drawbacks of excessive reliance on algorithm challenges.

- [Fizz Buzz Test](https://wiki.c2.com/?FizzBuzzTest) - Describes a simple screening task with the provocative claim that 99.5% of programming candidates cannot solve basic problems. That percentage is the source’s assertion.

- [FizzBuzz 2.0: Pragmatic Programming Questions for Software Engineers](https://web.archive.org/web/20211020130141/https://triplebyte.com/blog/fizzbuzz-2-0-pragmatic-programming-questions-for-software-engineers) - Presents five multiple-choice questions that the source proposes for distinguishing software-engineering skills.

- [FizzBuzz Enterprise Edition](https://github.com/EnterpriseQualityCoding/FizzBuzzEnterpriseEdition) - A satire of excessive engineering in the name of enterprise-grade software.

- [Awesome Interviews](https://github.com/MaximAbramchuck/awesome-interview-questions) - A large database of interview questions organized by topic.

### Negotiation

Negotiation is a critical final step in hiring.

- [How Not to Bomb Your Offer Negotiation](https://haseebq.com/how-not-to-bomb-your-offer-negotiation/) - Describes effective negotiators as empathetic and collaborative: they seek creative ways to meet both parties’ needs instead of controlling the other party or issuing ultimatums.

- [How to answer the “What's your current salary?” job interview question](https://web.archive.org/web/20190228034111/https://42hire.com/how-to-answer-the-whats-your-current-salary-job-interview-question-486254cb59ad?gi=a7f878096392) - Explains why interviewers ask about current salary and how a candidate can respond.

- [Salary Negotiation: Make More Money, Be More Valued](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2012/01/23/salary-negotiation/) - The author argues that salary negotiation, often concluded in under five minutes, can have a disproportionate effect on compensation.

- [Ten Rules for Negotiating a Job Offer](https://haseebq.com/my-ten-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer/) - The first part covers how to understand and begin negotiation and prepare for success; the second covers the exchange itself and how to ask for what a candidate wants.

## Onboarding

Onboarding involves both helping newcomers catch up with a team and introducing a manager to a team they have just joined or inherited.

- [The Most Important Performance Management Rule For Software Engineers](https://staysaasy.com/startups/2022/04/03/performance-management.html) - Proposes telling new software-engineering hires to merge code every week.

- [Optimize Onboarding](https://staysaasy.com/management/2020/08/28/Optimize-Onboarding.html) - Argues that long HR videos, slow security processes, and fragile technical setup impede newcomers. The goal of onboarding is to let people begin the work they were hired to do.

- [As a manager of a new employee I make an absolute point of being a "helicopter mom" from the moment they hit the area until about week 2 or 3](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24404676) - A manager’s account of close support during a new employee’s first two or three weeks, when navigating an unfamiliar organization can be difficult.

- [A Career Cold Start Algorithm](https://boz.com/articles/career-cold-start) - A method the author developed to get up to speed after joining a team with little relevant knowledge and no existing relationships.

- [Meeting everyone on a new team](https://www.annashipman.co.uk/jfdi/meeting-everyone.html) - After taking responsibility for an organization of 50 engineers, the author met everyone in 30-minute one-to-ones. Despite the time investment and a fear that the meetings would be boring, they helped reveal patterns in what needed to change.

## Motivation

- [Drive: The surprising truth about what motivates us](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6XAPnuFjJc) - Daniel Pink describes autonomy, mastery, and purpose as the drivers of motivation.

- [Bryan Cantrill’s account of management](https://x.com/bcantrill/status/1216491615264489473?s=20) emphasizes creating an environment for autonomy, mastery, and purpose rather than micromanaging it. He argues that poor engineering performance likely reflects a management problem: the wrong problem, mission, team, or a combination of the three.

- [What Silicon Valley "Gets" about Software Engineers that Traditional Companies Do Not](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/what-silicon-valley-gets-right-on-software-engineers/) - Describes seven practices: autonomy; treating engineers as curious problem-solvers rather than interchangeable resources; transparent internal data, code, and documentation; exposure to business goals and metrics; direct engineer-to-engineer communication rather than routing messages through intermediaries; investment in a less frustrating developer experience; and greater autonomy and pay for higher-leverage work.

- [Some reasons why enterprise software is good and maybe even fun](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21231455) - The original list argues that most developers are statistically more likely to build enterprise software than the next unicorn, and that enterprise work may be more interesting than expected.

### Happiness

- [First, Break All the Rules: What the World's Greatest Managers Do Differently](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/1595621113) - The original list reads the book as finding no correlation between employee happiness and company success. An HN comment describes [questions that were highly correlated to company success](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20571219).

- A [team member’s account](https://x.com/dwc/status/962179099606200320) describes tracking “life impact” through pages outside business hours and working to reduce them to zero. The list suggests that minimal out-of-hours disruption may be the best indicator of a happy team.

- [6 Signs You're a Micromanager (And What to Do Instead)](https://unito.io/blog/micromanagement-signs/) - Identifies possible micromanagement when closer supervision coincides with unhappy, less productive employees, unappreciated check-ins, and resistance to feedback.

### Procrastination

- [3 tricks to start working despite not feeling like it](https://www.deprocrastination.co/blog/3-tricks-to-start-working-despite-not-feeling-like-it) - Three suggestions for getting started: decide to do it, accept an imperfect start, and start small.

- [Why procrastination is about managing emotions, not time](https://www.bbc.com/worklife/article/20200121-why-procrastination-is-about-managing-emotions-not-time) - Discusses procrastination as an emotional issue and reports research suggesting that follow-through becomes easier after taking the first step.

## Culture

- [hacker-laws](https://github.com/dwmkerr/hacker-laws) - Laws, theories, principles, and patterns relevant to developers.

- [Adaptation vs adaptability](https://sci-hub.st/10.1016/s0303-2647%2801%2900170-8) - Explores the spectrum between efficiency and flexibility through material and energy flows between organisms in ecosystems. Recommended in an [HN comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20963513).

- [The IT revolution and southern Europe's two lost decades](https://voxeu.org/article/it-revolution-and-southern-europes-two-lost-decades) - Argues that inefficient management practices prevented southern European firms from fully benefiting from the IT revolution, illustrating how management culture can affect an industry.

- [Meaningful differences that makes Google offices more productive](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20443133) - A commenter’s account of Google offices, emphasizing capable colleagues, supportive management at multiple levels, and ease of moving between roles.

- [It's Not Enough to Be Right—You Also Have to Be Kind](https://archive.ph/RoW6v) - Argues that kindness is harder than cleverness. It also relates a saying attributed to Abraham Joshua Heschel about admiring intelligence when young and kindness when older.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7179946) argues that employees need not shield others, especially senior managers, from the consequences of their decisions. They advise acting in one’s own interests and placing responsibility on the organization to align those interests with its own.

## Engineering

Managers may no longer work as individual engineers, but their teams remain responsible for systems, technology, and the processes around them. Understanding engineering principles remains part of the role.

### The Technical Engineering Manager

The original list argues that a manager’s value lies beyond spending their time coding: coding can be left to the engineers, while the manager still needs technical understanding. These resources explore that distinction.

- [Do engineering managers need to be technical?](https://increment.com/teams/do-engineering-managers-need-to-be-technical/) - Argues that engineering managers need technical competence, while predicting that what counts as “technical” will continue to change over the next 30 years.

- [If Your Boss Could Do Your Job, You're More Likely to Be Happy at Work](https://archive.ph/J2vlo#selection-1025.8-1025.64) - Reports a study in which a boss’s technical competence was more strongly related to workplace happiness than occupation, education, tenure, or industry, although those factors also mattered.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23891984) recalls that the best managers could, if needed, do the work of people two levels below them. The list takes this as an argument for domain knowledge and understanding direct reports’ work.

- A [source discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20683609) cites [David Packard](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0887307477), HP’s co-founder, on supervisors thoroughly understanding their groups’ work. The list notes that the idea predates contemporary [management fads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_fad).

### Systems Complexity

Regardless of the technology stack, engineering involves building systems and managing their complexity.

- [Second-system effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-system_effect) - The tendency for a small, elegant, successful system to be replaced by an over-engineered, bloated successor.

- [Living with Complexity, by Donald A. Norman](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0262014866) - Discusses [Tesler’s law of the conservation of complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_conservation_of_complexity): simplifying a person’s interaction can move complexity behind the scenes, and simplifying one part can make another more complex. The principle treats total system complexity as conserved.

- [The Efficiency-Destroying Magic of Tidying Up](https://flocrivello.com/the-efficiency-destroying-magic-of-tidying-up/) - Argues that efficiency can look messy while visual tidiness can reduce efficiency, suggesting that sometimes [the messiness of the world](https://github.com/kdeldycke/awesome-falsehood) must be accepted.

- [I try to optimize my code around reducing state, coupling, complexity and code, in that order](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11042400) - An engineer’s stated priority order for robustness: reduce state, coupling, complexity, and then code.

- [SpaceX's 5-Step design and manufacturing process](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t705r8ICkRw&t=13m30s) - Five steps, in order: question and improve requirements, try to delete parts, simplify or optimize, accelerate cycle time, and automate. A [full transcript](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28517976) is also linked.

## Project Management

Product management addresses what to develop; project management addresses how to deliver it, including execution, the critical path, and planning.

Companies define these roles differently, and some combine them in hybrid positions.

- [Let's have no managers, instead of managers with no engineering experience](https://medium.com/hackernoon/lets-have-no-managers-instead-of-managers-with-no-engineering-experience-e8b7cd29d398) - Despite its broad title, the argument is specifically that project managers are unnecessary where product managers and scrum masters already cover the work.

- [Best project management practices in 2018?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16377523) - A discussion arguing that no single project-management practice is a universal solution.

- [Strategies for long Projects](http://benbrostoff.github.io/2019/09/28/long-projects.html) - Four strategies for long projects: persistent, irrational optimism; documenting daily progress; compounding investments; and budgeting time.

- [Developers can't fix bad management](https://iism.org/article/developers-can-t-fix-bad-management-57) - Compares software development to creating a new factory rather than running an existing one. It argues that tasks of unknown duration make traditional predictive management planning poorly suited to software projects.

### Specifications

- “Walking on water and developing software from a specification are easy if both are frozen.” Edward V. Berard - [Essays on object-oriented software engineering](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0132888955).

- [The art of destroying software](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ed94CfxgsCA) - Greg Young proposes designing code for deletion and keeping each part to at most one week’s coding effort, so any part can be rewritten within a week as requirements change.

- [Requirements volatility is the core problem of software engineering](https://stackoverflow.blog/2020/02/20/requirements-volatility-is-the-core-problem-of-software-engineering/) - Argues that change is inevitable, software is abandoned rather than definitively finished, and no software product is ever perfectly satisfactory.

### Estimations

Estimates support time management and planning, but can be treated as fixed deadlines.

- [Don't (guess)timate your projects, forecast with confidence](https://www.reaktor.com/blog/forecasting-method/) - Argues that extensive estimation can feel useful while being too inaccurate to provide business value. It advocates measurement and forecasting.

- [Dear Startup: You have no idea how much that costs](http://kyleprifogle.com/dear-startup/) - Questions confidence in estimating how long work will take and links a [way to respond to unreasonable estimation expectations](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21069178).

- [Escalation of commitment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalation_of_commitment) - Explains escalation of commitment and the sunk-cost fallacy: why people keep investing in a project despite evidence that it is going badly.

- [Who are you trying to impress with your deadlines?](https://archive.ph/9Hq7N) - Criticizes organizations that treat deadlines as immovable and regard a missed deadline as close to grounds for dismissal.

- [Apple Aperture: Senior QA](https://techreflect.net/2019/12/10/aperture-senior-qa-2004-2005/) - An account of deadline-driven management failures: removing finished features, yelling at staff, pushing people toward breakdown, and expecting over 100 engineers borrowed from other teams to solve the schedule problem.

- [Robert "Uncle Bob" Martin talk about professionalism in software development](https://youtu.be/LmRl0D-RkPU?t=3202) - Argues that honest uncertainty can be expressed through probability and risk assessments rather than a false promise. It compares this to PERT’s [optimistic, pessimistic and most-likely time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_evaluation_and_review_technique#Time). If managers reject a range, the talk suggests explaining that the team is already doing its best and making management responsible for the remaining risk.

- [Why software projects take longer than you think: a statistical model](https://erikbern.com/2019/04/15/why-software-projects-take-longer-than-you-think-a-statistical-model.html) - Presents a statistical model in which developers estimate the median duration reasonably well but the mean duration is substantially higher.

- [Developers spend most of their time figuring the system out](https://lepiter.io/feenk/developers-spend-most-of-their-time-figuri-9q25taswlbzjc5rsufndeu0py/) - Argues that hand-drawn representations of a system are beliefs that should be tested rather than accepted as engineering evidence. Because software is highly contextual, classes of problems may be predictable while specific problems are not. Understanding the assumptions behind a system is presented as a major reason effort is hard to estimate.

- [Software effort estimation is mostly fake research](https://shape-of-code.com/2021/01/17/software-effort-estimation-is-mostly-fake-research/) - Criticizes software-effort-estimation research based on small datasets: NASA has 93 rows, COCOMO 63, Desharnais 81, and the China dataset 499. The author stresses that NASA’s 93 is not missing a power of ten.

### Tickets

## Key Performance Indicator (KPI)

## Politics

Office politics sits at the intersection of power and influence. The original list links its nature and intensity to a company’s culture and history, and considers it hard to avoid beyond a certain management level.

- [About corporate middle management](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28336658) - A commenter describes corporate middle management as aligning people, managing friction, and making more senior managers look good.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22808280) describes office politics as middle managers creating distractions and barriers that prevent people below them from being seen by those closer to organizational power and money.

- [HiPPO FAQ](https://exp-platform.com/hippo/) - Explains HiPPO, “Highest Paid Person’s Opinion,” as a pattern in which organizational power overrides data.

- [The Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince) - Machiavelli’s account of acquiring honor and power as a leader. The list warns that adopting this kind of politics in a company can create a toxic, corrupting, and demoralizing culture.

- [The Gervais Principle](https://www.ribbonfarm.com/the-gervais-principle/) - A bleak, cynical interpretation of the management hierarchy using *The Office*.

- [The 48 Laws of Power](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0140280197) - Robert Greene’s book on power. The list presents it as a resource for protecting oneself and acting effectively in highly political organizations.

- [7 Rules of Power](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/1637741227) - Jeffrey Pfeffer’s book on power, discussed as [a guide to navigating office politics when workplace success is the only thing one cares about](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39084979).

- [Selectorate theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selectorate_theory) - A theory distinguishing three groups affecting a leader: the nominal selectorate, real selectorate, and winning coalition. It argues that retaining power requires maintaining the winning coalition.

- [Circulation of elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulation_of_elite) - A theory explaining changes of regime and revolutions through one elite replacing another, rather than rulers being overthrown from below.

- [The Rules for Rulers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rStL7niR7gs) - Describes key supporters monitoring alliances and switching allegiance when a ruler appears likely to lose. It presents securing loyalty as central to dictatorships and comparable organizations.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21925738) emphasizes the importance of political skill and sees it as missing from [key practices for achieving large professional goals](https://nodramadevops.com/2019/12/key-practices-for-achieving-large-professional-goals/).

- [“Company I've worked for had manager who tried to ship features over the weekend with a ragtag team of developers who don't understand why that's a bad idea.”](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22285123) - A commenter’s account of a manager rushing weekend feature releases with a makeshift team. - [Making Nice or Faking Nice? It suggests that organizations seeking authentic leadership consider candidates lower in “symbolized moral identity,” rather than selecting those higher in that trait.

- A [comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20260498) on [Why large companies are so difficult to rescue](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20260114) argues that the practical power of a senior executive tends to decrease as their organization grows.

- [4 Clues to Identify a Destructive Leader](https://www.tilt365.com/the-tilted-edge/blog/post/toxic-leadership-destructive-characteristics-examples) - Four warning signs: exaggerated self-importance, refusal to accept fault, demands for obedience, and claims of never being wrong.

- A [personal account](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1494369809538195456.html) attributes to an MIT president the view that tenure depends on departmental politics and being liked rather than research, productivity, or merit. The list uses it to caution that moving into academia does not necessarily escape organizational politics.

## Re-organizations

Managers shape team structure. The original list suggests that working directly with individual contributors becomes difficult beyond roughly eight to twelve reports, depending on the source, and may require restructuring.

Company-level restructuring is often strategic, with a manager’s influence depending partly on political judgment.

### Team-level

- [Why it's difficult to build teams in high growth organisations](https://jchyip.medium.com/why-its-difficult-to-build-teams-in-high-growth-organisations-e1aee8446337) - Three approaches to integrating new people during growth: “Sink or Swim,” “Split and Absorb,” and “Absorb and Split.”

- [Teams are like bread](https://blog.jessitron.com/2019/06/15/teams-are-like-bread/) - Echoes the “Absorb and Split” approach: nurture a team that works well, let it grow, and allow it to become a source of further strong teams without rushing or destroying it.

- [Building a data team at a mid-stage startup: a short story](https://erikbern.com/2021/07/07/the-data-team-a-short-story.html) - A manager’s account of growing a three-person data team while developing what it means to be a data-driven company. It follows changes in the technical pipeline and relationships with stakeholders.

- [If I Close My Data Centers, What About the People/Jobs Lost?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17329028) - A discussion of moving a Fortune 50 company’s data centers to a commercial cloud provider and what happens to staff maintaining legacy systems. It proposes retraining them.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20881308) criticizes attempts to replace employees’ judgment and competence with management processes, arguing that they fail. The list relates this to the case for retraining above.

- [I've Built Multiple Growth Teams. Here's Why I Won't Do It Again.](https://conversionxl.com/blog/dont-build-growth-teams/) - The author criticizes growth teams, arguing that few people understand probability and that most executives disregard what data says.

### Company-level

- [The SaaS Org Chart](https://archive.ph/vOuLQ) - Organizational blueprints at 50, 125, 400, and 1,000 employees, with typical staffing ratios and annual recurring revenue (ARR).

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20533922) argues that well-designed structures can keep even apparently incompetent large organizations operating. The list treats structure as central to organizational longevity.

- [A high-resilience org chart](https://jessitron.com/2021/05/26/a-high-resilience-org-chart/) - Distinguishes bureaucratic structures suited to known problems and solutions from resilient structures for generative work such as software development. It argues for fewer organizational boxes and greater flexibility.

- [An Alternative Approach to Re-Orgs At Your Company](https://caseyaccidental.com/alternative-approach-re-orgs/) - Describes an attempt to make restructuring driven by team feedback rather than instructions from above, using signals from daily work to expose inadequate structures. The list cautions that the approach may be limited by company culture.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21395557) argues that people responsible for past success can overshadow those who could create future success. They give the example of Netflix’s CEO excluding managers of the DVD-by-mail business from meetings planning the company’s streaming future.

- [Speaking Truth to Power: Reflections on My Career at Microsoft](https://onezero.medium.com/speaking-truth-to-power-reflections-on-a-career-at-microsoft-90f80a449e36) - After three decades at Microsoft, the author argues that leaders should reflect employees’ values, rather than force values downward. They favor changing leadership over speeches, training, and hashtags, replacing those successful under the old culture with leaders closer to employees in outlook and behavior.

### Acquisition

An acquisition is a special case of restructuring that can involve integrating, absorbing, or dissolving the acquired company.

- [How the Digg team was acquihired](https://lethain.com/digg-acquihire/) - An account of an acquihire as restructuring: managers may have to negotiate employment contracts for a whole team within one or two days. It warns that the emphasis on “stars” can put senior leaders under pressure from all sides unless they explicitly refuse to proceed.

## Health

- [Good sleep, good learning, good life](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Good_sleep,_good_learning,_good_life) - An ebook-length synthesis of sleep research aimed at practical use, especially by people who need high-quality sleep for learning and creative work.

### Holidays

- [Should we take a few long holidays, or lots of short ones?](http://timharford.com/2019/09/should-we-take-a-few-long-holidays-or-lots-of-short-ones/) - Argues for several short breaks: holiday memories depend more on the intensity of experiences than duration; changing activities can stimulate creativity; and regular rest is needed to prevent exhaustion, which one long holiday cannot provide.

### Stress

- [The Toxic Handler: Organizational Hero — and Casualty](https://www.companiesalive.com/toxichandlers-healthandhealing-lifecoaching-miami-leadershiptraining.htm) - Describes a “toxic handler” as a manager who voluntarily absorbs the sadness, frustration, bitterness, and anger of organizational life. Such people can appear at any level, though many work near the top.

- [Manager Energy Drain](https://larahogan.me/blog/manager-energy-drain/) - Three approaches to management fatigue: reorganize the calendar, delegate messy projects without clear scope, and say no.

- [How Slack Harms Projects](https://www.silasreinagel.com/blog/2019/08/12/how-slack-harms-projects/) - Argues that Slack can create false urgency, interrupt focus, bypass priorities, remove business context, and encourage poorly considered communication. It links [How to Use Slack and Not Go Crazy](https://pspdfkit.com/blog/2018/how-to-use-slack-and-not-go-crazy/) for an alternative approach.

- [Examples of harassments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21856352) - A commenter’s account of a jealous manager bullying a capable employee after feeling betrayed or ridiculed. The list presents it as behavior to avoid.

### Burnout

- [How shitty job crush your soul, then lead to burnout](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7789438) - A commenter describes severe burnout as potentially impairing effectiveness even in a later, otherwise good job, and compares taking it seriously to treating a physical injury.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5630618) disputes resentment alone as an explanation of burnout. They propose that repeated sacrifice and effort on high-risk problems ending in failure conditions the brain to associate work with failure, linking it to negative prediction errors in the nucleus accumbens. This is the commenter’s proposed mechanism.

- [If You're So Successful, Why Are You Still Working 70 Hours a Week?](https://archive.ph/78aEf) - Examines overwork and burnout as a combination of professional, organizational, and personal factors, with insecurity at the center.

- [What Happens When Your Career Becomes Your Whole Identity](https://archive.ph/yqPAD) - Discusses how high achievement, intense competition, and an overwork culture can entangle personal identity with a career and contribute to burnout.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21900054) recalls extreme workaholism as a way of avoiding or postponing unwanted life decisions, sometimes without conscious recognition. They warn that the consequences can arrive decades later.

- [Burnout From an Organizational Perspective](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/burnout_from_an_organizational_perspective) - Discusses toxic organizations and protecting teams from systemic burnout. Drawing on military research on sustainable performance under stress, it argues that leaders should advocate health rather than merely push tasks.

- [Avoiding burnout as an ambitious developer](https://stackoverflow.blog/2020/01/13/avoiding-burnout-as-an-ambitious-developer/) - Suggests being willing to say no, knowing what one does not want, using energy realistically, and being considerate toward one’s future self.

- [Psychology Today: How Programmers Can Avoid Burnout](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/dear-life-please-improve/202008/how-does-your-tech-job-burn-you-out) - Advice attributed to experienced developers: work where growth is possible, build transferable skills, make room for creativity and for switching off, and, as a drastic alternative, save enough money to leave.

- [Average tenure of a CISO is just 26 months due to high stress and burnout](https://www.zdnet.com/article/average-tenure-of-a-ciso-is-just-26-months-due-to-high-stress-and-burnout/) - A report citing an average CISO tenure of 26 months and describing low budgets, long hours, limited board authority, a shrinking hiring pool, continuing cyberattack and threat pressures, and little recognition for success alongside blame for failure.

## Setbacks and Failures

- “What does not kill me makes me stronger”, Friedrich Nietzsche - Brutal, but with a grain of truth.

- A [saying about survival through adaptability](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2014/05/04/adapt/) contrasts adaptation with strength or intelligence. Often misattributed to Charles Darwin, it derives from a later paraphrase associated with Leon C. Megginson. The list uses it to temper the preceding Nietzsche quotation.

- [Early-career setback and future career impact](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12189-3) - Reports that people with early-career near misses later outperformed those with narrow early wins, despite the initial setback.

- [Huge success in business is largely based on luck](https://theconversation.com/huge-success-in-business-is-largely-based-on-luck-new-research-130843) - Argues that management research and education should focus on helping practitioners move from incompetence to adequacy, rather than explaining exceptional success that may owe much to luck.

- [How Complex Systems Fail](https://stuff.mit.edu/afs/athena/course/2/2.75/resources/random/How%20Complex%20Systems%20Fail.pdf) - A short account of the nature of failure, its evaluation and attribution to proximate causes, and the implications for understanding patient safety.


- [Normalization of deviance](https://danluu.com/wat/) - Discusses how contributors to disasters can accumulate unnoticed until too late. The author contrasts research in other fields with what they see as a gap in software engineering.


- [Steve Jobs explains - Why companies fail?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B-fAinNDbQU&t=6s) - Steve Jobs discusses how sales and marketing can come to dominate product-centered companies.

- [The failure of Scaling Etsy](https://archive.ph/h07CR) - A critical account of missing technical leadership at Etsy: costly refactoring and over-engineering can distract developers from business and product needs.

## Exits

Resources on deciding to leave and understanding the effects of departures.

- [Why I Rejected My Manager](https://archive.ph/NxLVP) - An account illustrating the saying that people leave managers rather than companies.

- [Colleague is leaving. How to investigate what went wrong?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20786755) - A discussion arguing that departures often concern the boss rather than the work or company, and that exit interviews may yield little insight. A related [comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20787874) develops the point.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19755001) describes organizational meltdown through the observation that people become angry in clusters.


- A [personal account](https://danluu.com/wat/#fn:P) describes workplaces deteriorating after a VP leaves, revealing how much they had supported both direct reports and the wider organization.


- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21767843) suggests asking a valued manager or technical lead why they are leaving.

- An account of [business networks formed after group departures](https://archive.ph/9Osm2) describes people leaving for reasons beyond their individual performance: an acquisition at PayPal, years of underperformance at Tiger Management, an indictment at Drexel Burnham Lambert, and unrest and the British withdrawal from Aden affecting early Reliance employees. A [related source](https://archive.ph/WCxsu) presents group departures as potential beginnings for new ventures.

- A [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4324615) recalls collective departures and trends having an impact, while individual departures—even a founder’s—did not in their experience.

- [As an Employee, You Are Disposable](https://nelson.cloud/as-an-employee-you-are-disposable/) - Argues that liking a job or employer is compatible with recognizing that an employee can be replaced. A related [comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40943681) asks employees without significant ownership to examine why they are loyal and whether the employer reciprocates.

- [P.T.'s Hidden Meaning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yr4RvdREwl8) - The list interprets Hideo Kojima’s use of a playable teaser as a creative way to tell the story of turmoil at Konami before his departure despite an NDA. It notes that this strategy depends on the designer being influential and popular.

- [Management Challenges for the 21st Century - Managing Oneself](https://www.thecompleteleader.org/sites/default/files/imce/Managing%20Oneself_Drucker_HBR.pdf) - Drucker describes executive midlife crises as often involving boredom after reaching a career peak around age 45. Section V discusses why knowledge workers need to manage themselves and prepare for the second half of their lives.
